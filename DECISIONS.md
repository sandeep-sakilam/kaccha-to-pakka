2026-11-28 | How should a credit book (udhaar) be matched? | Per customer as a running balance: credit given adds, repayments (bank payments from the customer or written repayments) clear the oldest dues first within 90 days; exact clearings are applied before part payments, which count half | A due is never paid on the day it is written, and customers pay late, in lumps and in parts; one-to-one matching within 3 days cannot see any of that.
2026-11-28 | Should cash entries count against the match rate? | No: entries written as cash are left out of the match rate, but a ledger with more cash entries than checkable ones has its records score capped at 40 | Cash cannot be checked against a bank, but marking everything cash must not be a way to avoid checking.
2026-11-28 | What do Khatabook and OkCredit rows mean? | Customer statements are credit books (You Gave / Credit = credit given, You Got / Payment = money received); a Khatabook supplier statement's You Gave is a payment and its You Got (goods on credit) is left out | That is how both apps label their books; treating credit given as money paid out made every credit entry look unmatched.
2026-11-28 | A small UPI payment from a customer who still owes money: repayment or spot purchase? | Counted as a part payment (weaker, counts half), and reported as the known false positive in the credit-book eval | The money looks identical; the rules cannot tell them apart, so the weaker weight limits the harm and the eval says so openly.
2026-11-28 | How do we handle a customer paying from a relative's phone? | Same surname matches on its own; a different name is asked about once when it is the only candidate, and the owner's yes is remembered by that payment's UPI ID as well as by name | Names alone cannot prove family; the exact amount and date plus one owner answer can, and the UPI ID keeps working when the app shows a different name.
2026-11-28 | What about a UPI ID that is only a phone number, with no name? | Treated as no name to compare: matched only when it is the single candidate both ways, otherwise asked about with the number masked | Throwing these away lost real payments; the single-candidate rule is the one already used for ledger lines without a name.
2026-11-28 | Should the owner's own yes count like an automatic match? | It counts, but as its own rule; if it carries more than half the matched value the records score is capped at 60 and lenders see the share | An answer cannot create money, but a ledger whose matches mostly rest on the owner's word is weaker evidence.
2026-11-28 | Names with no word in common ('Sharma ji' / PRIYA VERMA scored 57) | Capped at 40 (below the question threshold) unless a word agrees; such a pair is asked about only when it is the single candidate | Character overlap between different words (sharma/verma) is not name evidence and produced noisy questions.
2026-11-28 | How should cash deposits count? | As sales at half weight in the money pillar's volume and monthly series, shown to lenders as their own line | Deposits show real trading, but borrowed cash can be deposited to look bigger; UPI from many payers is stronger.
2026-11-28 | When does the owner's spoken daily sales total count? | Only for months with 8+ days told, when the owner's average day is 0.8-3x the bank's average day (UPI and bank in plus cash deposited); 2+ agreeing months and none failing add 5 points to records, capped | It is self-reported: it may confirm what the bank shows, never replace it; the band is wide because cash sales come on top of what the bank sees.
2026-11-28 | Which further functionalities add the most weight? | F61 registration check, F62 verified business age, F63 address proof, F64 supplier credit, F65 woman helper, F66 passport history, F67 demo seed; not more languages (machine translation of consent text unchecked by native speakers), not IVR (needs a telephony provider), not GST return data (needs a licensed GST data provider) | Each chosen item either replaces a self-reported input with evidence, fills a SPEC promise, or makes the passport more useful to lenders; the excluded ones need people or licences we do not have.
2026-11-28 | Where do Udyam / GSTIN lookups come from? | The KYC provider adapter (same licensed provider and keys as PAN), with a Sandbox register for the demo | Providers that verify PAN also verify GSTIN and Udyam; one integration, one set of keys.
2026-11-28 | What does a registration name mismatch do? | Hold for review, like a PAN mismatch; cancelled only warns; verified adds 10 presence points | A number registered to someone else is borrowing an identity; a cancelled one is common after closing a GST registration and is not fraud.
2026-11-28 | How much should a claimed business age count? | Proven age (register date, Udyam commencement on a registration at least 6 months old, first Account Aggregator payment) counts fully; claimed age counts at half | Age was the largest self-reported input in the score; halving rather than zeroing keeps honest owners of old, unregistered shops from being punished while a made-up age gains little.
2026-11-28 | What does a bill in someone else's name do? | Not counted, owner told why; no hold | Shops are often rented and the electricity bill is in the landlord's name; treating that as fraud would punish ordinary tenants.
2026-11-28 | Can a shop licence prove business age? | Yes, its issue date when the licence is accepted and the AI read the date with confidence of at least 0.8 | A licence is a government document in the shop's name; the confidence floor keeps a misread date from inventing years.
2026-11-28 | How are supplier dues matched? | Exactly like customer credit, mirrored: payments out to the supplier clear the oldest purchases within 90 days, exact before partial | One rule set for both sides of a credit book is easier to explain and test; lenders read 'pays suppliers in N days' as payment discipline.
2026-11-28 | Does a spoken credit sentence mean given, bought or repaid? | Repayment words (wapas, mile, jama, chukaye) make it a payment; buying words (liya, aaya, kharida) make it goods bought on credit; otherwise credit given | Every credit sentence carries one of these verbs, and getting the side wrong would move the dues to the wrong book.
